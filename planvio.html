The product

This is the whole thing.
No mock-ups.

Every screen below is the live product running against a demo company — a services firm with sixteen projects, real hours booked against them, and invoices that reconcile. Click through the modules and see what your team would actually open on a Monday morning.

Where the money might come from. The funnel shows how many deals reached each stage and what share carried on, so you can see where you lose them rather than only what is left. Pipeline value is weighted by win probability, not the sum of every hopeful number on the list.

Pipeline → Dashboard

The plan, and whether it is still true. Tasks group into work packages with dates, hours and cost on every line. Submit the plan for review and it locks — from then on the baseline is what you measure drift against, instead of a number that quietly moved.

Planning → Schedule

Every project, on one screen, honestly. One row each: the bar is when the project actually runs, the mark is when it was due to finish. Late is visible as distance, not buried in a status field somebody forgot to update.

Delivery → Progress

Who is free, and when. A cell per person per week, shaded by how booked they are. Past weeks show hours actually logged; future weeks show what is committed. Approved leave is taken off the week it falls in, not smeared across the month.

Capacity → Capacity Pool

Who has filled their timesheet in. A week per person, shaded by hours, so a thin week is visible before payroll asks. Hours land against a task, which is what makes a project's cost and its margin move on their own — and unbilled hours are counted, not guessed at.

Time → Dashboard

Risk you can actually read. Likelihood against impact, counts in the squares, the dangerous corner where you expect it. Click a square and the register below filters to those risks — the matrix is a control, not a picture.

Assurance → Risk

What is owed, and how late. Unpaid invoices grouped by age, so the shape of what is outstanding is the first thing you see. Days to get paid comes from the last year's billing rather than a guess, and overdue is derived from the due date rather than trusted from a stale status.

Finance → Dashboard

Who you have, and how long they stay. Tenure by band, and who carries the reporting lines. Attrition reads as a dash until a leaving date exists — an empty column is not evidence that nobody has left. Pay, burden and bill rate stay three separate numbers, because treating them as one is how a project looks profitable until it is finished.

Questions we get asked

Is this only for construction or field companies?

No. Planvio was built to handle the hardest version of project work, which happens to involve crews, site records, and heavy cost tracking. That same structure fits software delivery, consulting, engineering, events, and manufacturing. You switch off the modules you do not need.

Do we need Microsoft 365 to use it?

For the edition available today, yes. Planvio runs on the Microsoft Power Platform, so each customer needs a Microsoft tenant and Power Platform licensing, which is billed by Microsoft. Planvio Cloud removes that requirement and is in development. Join the waitlist and we will price it for you first.

Does it connect to QuickBooks?

Yes, in both directions. Estimates, bills, and invoices push from Planvio into QuickBooks, and payment status, vendors, and customers come back. Hours can also be pushed to QuickBooks Time for payroll.

Can we control who sees what?

Yes. Access is controlled by a role matrix covering every screen and action, with nineteen role types out of the box and the ability to scope a user to only the projects they are assigned to. You can edit the matrix yourself, and preview the app as any role to check it.

Where does our data live?

In your own Microsoft Dataverse environment, inside your tenant, under your existing security and compliance settings. We do not hold a copy.

Can you migrate what we already have?

Yes. Projects, contacts, rates, and historical costs can be imported from spreadsheets, Smartsheet, or QuickBooks as part of setup. That import is usually the first thing we do, because a demo on your own data is far more convincing than a demo on ours.
